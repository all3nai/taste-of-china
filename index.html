02 — The rituals around the table

Beyond the food:
the art of Chinese dining.

In China, a meal is more than nourishment. It is hospitality, family, celebration and a language of care expressed through shared dishes.

01

The shared table

Many Chinese meals are served family-style, with dishes placed at the centre so everyone can take a little of everything. Sharing communicates warmth and belonging.

02

Chopsticks

More than utensils, chopsticks are part of a long dining tradition. They are used with care and have their own etiquette, especially at formal meals.

03

Tea, poured with care

Tea accompanies everyday meals and formal gatherings alike. Offering and refilling tea can be a quiet gesture of respect and hospitality.

04

The banquet

Formal banquets often feature a sequence of shared dishes, symbolic ingredients and a host who makes sure guests are welcomed and well fed.

05

New Year traditions

Foods carry wishes: dumplings can suggest wealth, fish represents abundance, and long noodles are associated with longevity.

06

North & south

Wheat-based foods such as noodles and dumplings are prominent in much of the north; rice is central in many southern food traditions. Local variation is immense.

THE LANGUAGE OF THE TABLE · 餐桌礼仪
A small guide to good manners

Chopsticks
with consideration.

DO

Rest chopsticks neatly on a holder or across your bowl when taking a pause.

DO

Offer food to elders or guests when appropriate, using serving utensils where provided.

DON'T

Do not stand chopsticks upright in a bowl of rice; this resembles incense at a funeral.

DON'T

Avoid pointing with chopsticks, tapping bowls, or using them to search through shared dishes.

DON'T

Do not pass food from chopsticks to chopsticks; this echoes a funeral ritual.

03 — A living heritage

One country, many culinary worlds.

Chinese food culture cannot be reduced to a single flavour. Geography, climate, trade, migration and family traditions have shaped distinct regional cuisines. This digital exhibition is an invitation to explore that diversity—one dish, one city, one shared table at a time.

A note on the journey

Food is a living story.

Recipes and customs evolve across families, cities and generations. The regional descriptions here are introductions—not rigid rules—and many dishes have multiple origin stories or local variations.
